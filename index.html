AI that evaluates its own judgment, asks for help, and improves from feedback

My research asks three connected questions: can a model tell when its own judgment is wrong, when should it defer or ask for help, and how can it learn from the feedback it gets? I answer them with self-evaluation, calibration, and distribution-free guarantees for LLM and agentic systems, built on mathematics and information theory.

About

Modern AI systems should not only answer. They should evaluate their own judgments, ask for help when those judgments are unreliable, and improve from the feedback they receive.

I study how AI systems reason, quantify uncertainty, and improve under limited supervision. At AWS, I develop methods that let large language models assess their own outputs, defer or ask when that assessment is weak, and turn feedback into better behavior.

My research combines mathematical rigor with practical AI systems, spanning LLM self-evaluation, Bayesian uncertainty, active learning, and information-theoretic foundations. I wrote down how these threads came together in From Calibration to Self-Improvement.

Before AWS, I worked at Samsung SDS Research America and Moody’s Analytics. I hold a Ph.D. in Applied Mathematics from Yale, a B.S. in Mathematics from KAIST, and was a Simons Postdoctoral Fellow at UT Austin.

My goal is to bridge mathematical rigor and real-world AI systems in ways that make advanced models more trustworthy and useful.

Selected Work

EMNLP 2026 SAC Highlight Q2 · Defer

From Debate to Decision: Conformal Social Choice for Safe Multi-Agent Deliberation

Key finding Debate can talk agents into unanimous wrong answers (23.9% of initially disputed MMLU-Pro questions by round 3). A split-conformal layer on the agents’ pooled probabilities acts only on singleton prediction sets and escalates the rest, with a distribution-free coverage guarantee.

MF Wang, H Xie, G Wang, A Gao, G Yang, Z Li, QW Qiu, F Han, H Qiu, Y Huang, B Zhu, JO Woo
Paper Workshop arXiv Essay
EMNLP 2026 ICLR 2026 Workshop Best Paper (Workshop) Q1 · Self-evaluation

Structural Uncertainty from Self-Preference in Black-Box Language Models

Key finding Sampled answers can agree while the model’s own pairwise preferences among them keep changing. Measuring that instability, split into within-trial and across-trial parts, flags errors that answer-dispersion baselines miss and combines well with them.

B Chaudhury, MF Wang, HH Park, R Ghosh, S Hong, JO Woo
Paper Workshop Essay
ACM CAIS 2026 Workshop Q2 · Ask for help

Knowing When to Ask: Self-Gated Clarification for Hierarchical Language Agents

Key finding Rating “ask” on the same scale as navigation lets help-seeking appear at intermediate decision points. On tariff classification across 9 LLMs, the share of questions followed by a correct next step rises from 50% to 74%, and where agents ask stays stable when answer quality is degraded.

A Gao, Y Kang, MF Wang, JO Woo
Paper Workshop Essay
EACL 2026 Findings Q3 · Improve

Reflect, Rewrite, Repeat: How Simple Arithmetic Enables Advanced Reasoning in Small Language Models

Key finding A stronger teacher model grades and rewrites a small model’s answers, and the small model learns from those corrections through iterative DPO, a verification step, and two-stage SFT. With about 700 arithmetic training samples, Qwen 2.5 7B base models improve on GSM8K (83.1 → 88.6), AIME’25@10 (20.0 → 30.0), and LastLetterConcat (40.7 → 53.3).

MF Wang, H Xie, MY Kim, B Chaudhury, M Ashok, S Gunturu, S Hong, JO Woo
Paper Code
NeurIPS 2025 Workshop Q1 · Self-evaluation

Can LLMs Reliably Evaluate Themselves? A Probabilistic VC Framework

Key finding Treats self-evaluation as a hypothesis class with a probabilistic VC capacity and sample-complexity bounds (a theoretical result under stated assumptions). In per-category audits against an LLM judge reference, no 7 to 8B model we tested was best both at picking its better solutions and at keeping its confidence honest.

JO Woo, MF Wang, R Ghosh, B Chaudhury, MY Kim
Paper Essay
ICML 2024 Q3 · Improve

Improving Instruction Following in Language Models through Proxy-Based Uncertainty Estimation

Key finding Preference data and reward judgments are themselves uncertain. An uncertainty-aware reward model scores response pairs and estimates the uncertainty of each judgment with Bayesian approximation; using that uncertainty to curate training data and in the policy optimization objective improves instruction following on benchmarks such as Vicuna and MT-bench.

J Lee, JO Woo, J Seok, P Hassanzadeh, W Jang, et al.
Paper arXiv Code
ICLR 2023 Top 25% Samsung Best Paper (Gold) Foundation

Active Learning in Bayesian Neural Networks with Balanced Entropy Learning Principle

Key finding A balanced-entropy acquisition rule, derived from information-theoretic first principles, avoids the pull of standard acquisition functions toward samples that are either too confident or too uncertain. The principle was later transferred into 4 issued US patents at Samsung.

JO Woo
Paper arXiv Code
IEEE ISIT 2022 Foundation

Analytic Mutual Information in Bayesian Neural Networks

Key finding Derives a closed-form expression for mutual information in Bayesian neural networks, replacing the expensive approximations usually needed to compute it.
